# Negative prompt: reject generic AI product patterns

Treat this as a review filter, not a universal style ban. A pattern may remain only when content, brand, task, or evidence gives it a specific purpose.

## Visual patterns to reject by default

- Generic purple/blue gradients, glowing blobs, aurora backgrounds, and decorative grid noise.
- Glassmorphism, excessive blur, floating translucent cards, and shadows without hierarchy or spatial meaning.
- A giant interchangeable headline centered above two pill buttons and a dashboard mockup.
- Bento grids used as a default page structure regardless of the content relationship.
- Rounded containers around every section, excessive pills, and uniform card grids that erase hierarchy.
- Random icons, emoji decoration, sparkles, rockets, magic wands, and illustrations unrelated to the product domain.
- Oversized typography that consumes the first mobile viewport without helping the user's task.
- Decorative charts, fake activity feeds, invented avatars, fake notifications, and meaningless metric counters.
- Repeated alternating feature rows, three-column benefit blocks, and pricing layouts copied from generic SaaS templates.
- Motion on every element, scroll-jacking, gratuitous parallax, and animation that delays comprehension.

## Language patterns to reject

- “Revolutionize,” “supercharge,” “unlock,” “seamless,” “effortless,” “next-generation,” and similar unsupported claims.
- Vague headlines that could describe any product.
- Fake testimonials, customer counts, percentages, awards, partner logos, or trust badges.
- Explanations of features without the user consequence or context.
- Multiple calls to action with equal visual weight.
- Placeholder copy presented as final content.

## Product patterns to reject

- Building screens before the primary user job and success condition are clear.
- Adding dashboards where a decision, task queue, or focused answer would be more useful.
- Exposing database structure instead of the user's mental model.
- Shipping disconnected mock screens or buttons with no real behavior as a “complete platform.”
- Copying competitor information architecture without validating user context.
- Adding AI chat, recommendations, or generation without a defined job, failure mode, and human control.
- Optimizing visual novelty while leaving empty, error, permission, recovery, and destructive states undefined.

## Replacement questions

For every rejected pattern, ask:

1. What user decision or action should this element support?
2. What domain-specific content deserves emphasis?
3. What evidence makes this claim truthful?
4. Could this layout belong unchanged to another product? If yes, make it more specific or simpler.
5. What can be removed without reducing meaning or capability?
