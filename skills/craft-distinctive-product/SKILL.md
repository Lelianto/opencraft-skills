# Craft Distinctive Product

Make every product decision express user meaning, domain character, and deliberate hierarchy. Originality must improve recognition and use—not become decoration.

## Workflow

1. Read the product evidence, target user, job, context of use, content, brand assets, and existing design language.
2. State the product's meaningful core in one sentence: who it helps, what consequential change it enables, and what should feel uniquely true about the experience.
3. Derive a design thesis from domain-specific material such as language, workflows, data, physical references, cultural context, or customer behavior. Do not derive it from current template trends alone.
4. Choose one or two signature ideas—interaction, composition, typography, information treatment, or motion—and keep the rest disciplined so the signature remains legible.
5. Apply the negative-pattern review in [references/negative-patterns.md](references/negative-patterns.md). Remove any pattern that lacks a product-specific reason.
6. For mobile web, follow [references/mobile-app-quality.md](references/mobile-app-quality.md). Recompose tasks, navigation, density, actions, and overlays for touch and constrained attention; do not merely stack desktop columns.
7. Write specific product language. Replace inflated claims, generic headings, invented metrics, and placeholder testimonials with truthful content or clearly labeled placeholders.
8. Verify hierarchy, readability, accessibility, responsive behavior, performance, and real content extremes. Distinctiveness never overrides usability.
9. Explain the meaning behind consequential visual choices and identify what makes the result recognizably specific to this product.

## Output contract

Return or implement:

- a concise product meaning and design thesis;
- the retained signature ideas and their rationale;
- rejected generic patterns and replacements;
- mobile-specific composition and behavior;
- evidence from representative states and viewports;
- unresolved originality, usability, or content risks.

## Guardrails

- Do not imitate a named living designer, brand, or competitor closely.
- Do not confuse novelty with arbitrary visual noise.
- Do not fabricate customer evidence, usage statistics, logos, awards, or social proof.
- Do not hide weak product value behind polish.
- Preserve an established design system unless a deliberate redesign is in scope.
- Use `facilitate-product-decision` when choosing among material design theses or signature interactions. Explain the product meaning, mobile behavior, originality risks, accessibility, and implementation consequences of each direction.
